# Product

<!-- impeccable:product-schema 1 -->

## Platform

web

## Users

Students of the Colegio de Ciencias y Humanidades (CCH-UNAM) using course materials and learning activities, often from a mobile device.

## Product Purpose

PEL-25 is the CCH-UNAM online study-program platform. It gives students access to subject content organized by learning units and helps them complete the learning activities associated with their courses.

## Positioning

An institutional CCH-UNAM study platform that brings course content and Moodle-compatible learning resources together in a mobile-first experience.

## Operating Context

Students navigate subjects, units, and topics to read course materials and use Moodle learning resources such as H5P activities, exams, and forums. The platform is used on phones as well as larger screens.

## Capabilities and Constraints

- Content is organized by subject, unit, and topic.
- The platform uses PHP, JavaScript, Tailwind CSS, and Webpack.
- It integrates or links to Moodle-compatible resources, including H5P activities, exams, and forums.
- Mobile-first navigation and resource use are a product priority.

## Brand Commitments

- Preserve the institutional identity of Escuela Nacional Colegio de Ciencias y Humanidades, UNAM.
- Use Spanish as the primary interface and content language.

## Evidence on Hand

- Existing subject content, images, H5P packages, and activity files are in `src/`.
- Navigation and institutional legal/social links are defined in `src/menu.json`.
- No additional product-specific accessibility standard has been confirmed.

## Product Principles

- Make it easy for students to find where they are and move through course content on a phone.
- Keep interactive learning resources reachable and usable in the course flow.
- Maintain the accuracy and institutional context of CCH-UNAM educational materials.
- Support learning activities without making students manage unnecessary interface complexity.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Mobile-first usage is a confirmed priority. Specific accessibility requirements remain to be defined.
